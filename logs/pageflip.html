PageFlip — Assistive Device

A first-year design project: a hand-fitted aid for people with limited hand mobility, built around a rubber tip for flipping pages and a replaceable handle that swaps in a fork or pen. It slides into the palm and is supported by the crease between index and thumb, so no finger movement is needed. My CAD model was the one the group took forward, and it undercuts roughly $200 industry equivalents.

The project: design an assistive device for people with mobility issues.

1. handling our problem

Upon research, we decided that the problem we wanted to tackle was to help people with hand mobility issues. Our target was to provide a versatile solution so that the person relying on the device can use it for multiple purposes. There were several brainstorming stages, where we each sketched ideas and then created CAD models of them. In the end upon final judgement, my solution was the one we referred to, which had a rubber tip that could help flip pages, a replaceable handle to switch for use with fork or pen adjustments. The device would easily slide into the user palm so that finger movement was not an issue (it would be supported by the crease between the index and thumb). Beyond this, we made sure it was really light at 51 g and cost around $20 to 3D print. It was designed geometrically so that one affordable solution could last the user for years, beating industry competition that average around $200.

2. pitching

We obviously had to pitch our solution, so that investors would buy in. In 60 seconds, me and Kourosh delivered our elevator pitch which stood out receiving no negative feedback from the judges and lots of praise. However, in a small moment it is difficult to stand out, and hence for our more interested audience, we designed a website for PageFlip that comprehensively explained its design, function and comparison to competition. We ended the project semester on a high, and definitely raised my interest for modelling.
