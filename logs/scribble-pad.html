Scribble Pad

A drawing and annotation tool built in Processing after Zoom locked students out of annotating during online class. Around 700 lines of Java, written without AI, with colour options, switchable boards and saving. It took a podium at The Tech Lab Programming Competition, and now runs in the browser too.

2021
JavaProcessingp5.js

0. where it started

Scribbling noises as I continued to annotate on zoom while my patient teacher kept taking class without a show of dissatisfaction. During covid, the phenomenon of online classes were quite unique to me. It wasn’t ideal, since I kept losing focus to my greater attraction on the tools in zoom, especially the annotation. But I couldn’t always keep annotating once a new update rolled around, allowing instructors to restrict screen annotation during class. It is exactly during this period that I figured I needed a new annotation tool (Microsoft Paint was nothing special and very child unfriendly!).

1. processing

The course was conducted on Processing, which was a programming software, by default for Java (not that other languages weren’t adaptable. What made this special, was this was a editor designed primarily for the front end, and was used to make soft concept video games or softwares. Hence, it was quite user friendly, and you could overtake the complexities of something like Visual Code (the terminal and what not) and focus on seeing your results with the click of the “play” button.

2. brainstorm

My idea for this project was essentially to somewhat replicate the Zoom annotation experience (which was quite frankly very responsive and had a lot of easy options). I managed to add a few more things, as my young brain felt necessary and I got to work.

3. exhaustive coding (WITHOUT AI WHAT)

I can barely imagine coding even 50 lines nowadays. But writing 700 lines of codes… whoa ok that is never happening again. The things covid gets you to do huh. With the help of my instructor Noor, I was able to ensure tracking the mouse's movements on the software, and getting left-click feedback for writing and right-click feedback for erasing. The UI also allows for changing colors, changing boards and even saving your work. After several attempts, I finally got my project working. Pffpt, never again! Thankfully though, the project was original enough and executed amazingly, and was able to secure a podium at The Tech Lab Programming Competition!

Make sure to check out the web version I made using p5.js, it replicates all the functionalities.
